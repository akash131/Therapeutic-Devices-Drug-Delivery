# Rehabilitation Devices

Rehabilitation devices assist individuals in recovering, maintaining, or improving physical function following injury, illness, or congenital conditions. These technologies range from passive assistive devices to advanced powered systems that restore mobility and independence.

## Powered Prosthetics

### Overview
Powered prosthetics are artificial limbs that use motors, actuators, and electronic control systems to restore function for individuals with limb loss or limb difference.

### Upper Limb Prosthetics

#### Myoelectric Prostheses
Control systems that detect electrical signals from residual limb muscles.

**Components:**
- Surface electrodes (EMG sensors)
- Microprocessor controller
- Battery system
- Electric motors/actuators
- Prosthetic hand/terminal device

**Control Strategies:**
| Strategy | Description | Complexity |
|----------|-------------|------------|
| Direct Control | One muscle = one movement | Basic |
| Two-site | Flexor/extensor pair | Standard |
| Pattern Recognition | Machine learning classification | Advanced |
| TMR | Targeted muscle reinnervation | Surgical + advanced |

**Available Hand Designs:**
- Multi-articulating hands (Ottobock bebionic, Ossur i-Limb)
- Split hook designs
- Task-specific terminal devices
- Partial hand prostheses

#### Body-Powered Prostheses
- Cable-operated mechanical systems
- Harness captures body movements
- Durable and proprioceptive feedback
- Lower cost, less maintenance

### Lower Limb Prosthetics

#### Microprocessor Knees (MPK)
Computerized knee joints that adapt to walking conditions.

**Features:**
- Real-time gait analysis
- Stumble recovery algorithms
- Variable cadence adaptation
- Stair and ramp modes
- Sitting/standing transitions

**Examples:**
- Ottobock C-Leg, Genium, X3
- Ossur Rheo Knee
- Freedom Innovations Plié
- Endolite Orion

#### Powered Knee-Ankle Systems
- Active propulsion during gait
- Reduced metabolic cost
- Stair climbing capability
- Example: Ottobock Empower, BionX

#### Microprocessor Feet
- Adaptive ankle movement
- Terrain accommodation
- Slope adjustment
- Examples: Ottobock Meridium, Ossur Proprio Foot

### Osseointegration
- Direct skeletal attachment
- Eliminates socket interface
- Improved proprioception
- OPRA, Compress systems

### Emerging Technologies
- Sensory feedback systems (haptic, electrical)
- Brain-computer interface control
- Regenerative peripheral nerve interfaces
- 3D-printed custom components

## Exoskeletons

### Overview
Wearable robotic devices that augment, enhance, or restore human movement through external structural support and powered assistance.

### Classification

#### By Application

**Medical/Rehabilitation Exoskeletons:**
- Gait training for spinal cord injury
- Stroke rehabilitation
- Neurological condition support
- Over-ground ambulation

**Industrial/Occupational Exoskeletons:**
- Lifting assistance
- Overhead work support
- Repetitive task augmentation
- Fatigue reduction

**Military/Defense:**
- Load carrying augmentation
- Endurance enhancement
- Tactical applications

#### By Body Region
- **Full-body**: Complete lower and upper extremity
- **Lower limb**: Hip, knee, ankle joints
- **Upper limb**: Shoulder, elbow, wrist
- **Trunk/Back**: Spinal support systems

### Medical Exoskeletons

#### Rehabilitation Systems

**ReWalk (Lifeward):**
- FDA-cleared for personal and rehabilitation use
- Hip and knee motorized joints
- Crutch-based balance
- Tilt sensor gait initiation

**Ekso Bionics (EksoNR):**
- Rehabilitation-focused design
- Variable assist modes
- Real-time biofeedback
- Clinical gait training protocols

**Indego (Parker Hannifin):**
- Modular lightweight design
- Functional mobility focus
- Home and community use
- Therapy integration

**HAL (Hybrid Assistive Limb - Cyberdyne):**
- Bioelectric signal control
- Voluntary movement detection
- Neuroplasticity enhancement
- Available in multiple configurations

#### Clinical Applications
- Spinal cord injury (complete and incomplete)
- Stroke recovery
- Multiple sclerosis
- Traumatic brain injury
- Cerebral palsy (pediatric systems)

### Control Systems

#### Sensing Technologies
- EMG (electromyography)
- Force/torque sensors
- Inertial measurement units (IMU)
- Joint angle encoders
- Ground reaction force sensors

#### Control Strategies
| Type | Description | User Input |
|------|-------------|------------|
| Triggered | Preset patterns initiated | Button/sensor trigger |
| Assistive | Augments user effort | Detect intended movement |
| Adaptive | Adjusts to conditions | Continuous feedback |
| Autonomous | Independent operation | Minimal user input |

### Considerations
- Fitting and customization requirements
- Training program duration
- Energy expenditure comparison
- Contraindications (severe osteoporosis, contractures)
- Regulatory pathway (FDA Class II)

## Functional Electrical Stimulation (FES) Systems

### Overview
FES systems apply electrical currents to paralyzed or weakened muscles to produce functional movement, restore motor function, or prevent muscle atrophy.

### Mechanism of Action
- Electrical stimulation of lower motor neurons
- Muscle fiber recruitment (type II before type I)
- Coordinated multi-muscle activation
- Programmed stimulation sequences

### System Components
- Stimulator unit (external or implanted)
- Electrodes (surface, percutaneous, or implanted)
- Control interface (switches, sensors, EMG)
- Power source

### Surface FES Systems

#### FES Cycling
- Lower extremity ergometer systems
- Cardiovascular conditioning
- Muscle strengthening
- Bone density maintenance
- RT300 (Restorative Therapies), MOTOmed systems

#### Drop Foot Stimulators
- Peroneal nerve stimulation
- Swing phase dorsiflexion
- Heel switch or tilt sensor triggering
- Examples: Bioness L300, Ottobock ActiGait, Odstock ODFS

#### Upper Extremity FES
- Hand grasp restoration
- Reach and grasp training
- Stroke rehabilitation applications
- Examples: Bioness H200, MyndMove

### Implanted FES Systems

#### Freehand System (Historical)
- First FDA-approved implanted FES
- C5-C6 tetraplegia hand grasp
- Shoulder position control

#### StimRouter
- Minimally invasive lead placement
- External pulse transmitter
- Peripheral nerve targeting

#### Networked Neuroprosthesis
- Multi-function restoration
- Standing, transfers, stepping
- Research and limited clinical use

### Clinical Applications

| Application | Target Population | Function Restored |
|-------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Walking | Incomplete SCI, stroke | Ambulation assistance |
| Cycling | Complete/incomplete SCI | Exercise, cardiovascular |
| Hand grasp | Tetraplegia, stroke | Object manipulation |
| Standing | Paraplegia | Transfers, standing frame |
| Bladder | SCI | Voiding control |
| Respiration | High cervical SCI | Diaphragm pacing |

### FES Exercise Benefits
- Muscle mass preservation
- Cardiovascular fitness
- Bone density maintenance
- Pressure ulcer prevention
- Spasticity reduction
- Psychological benefits

### Contraindications
- Demand pacemakers (relative)
- Lower motor neuron injury
- Denervated muscles
- Severe contractures
- Skin breakdown at electrode sites

## Emerging Rehabilitation Technologies

### Brain-Computer Interfaces (BCI)
- EEG-based motor imagery detection
- Invasive electrode arrays
- Robotic arm/exoskeleton control
- Communication restoration

### Virtual Reality Rehabilitation
- Immersive therapy environments
- Gamification of exercises
- Upper extremity stroke recovery
- Balance and gait training

### Soft Robotics
- Textile-based exosuits
- Lightweight, compliant designs
- Natural movement patterns
- Harvard Biodesign Lab research

### Regenerative Technologies
- Stem cell therapies (adjunct)
- Nerve conduits and guides
- Bioelectronic medicine
- Neuromodulation integration

## Prescription and Training Considerations

### Assessment Requirements
- Physical examination and functional evaluation
- Goals identification (patient-centered)
- Environmental and social factors
- Cognitive and psychological readiness

### Multidisciplinary Team
- Physiatrist/rehabilitation physician
- Physical therapist
- Occupational therapist
- Prosthetist/orthotist
- Rehabilitation engineer
- Psychologist/counselor

### Training Protocols
- Device-specific operation training
- Progressive skill development
- Home exercise programs
- Community integration practice
- Long-term follow-up schedule

### Funding and Access
- Insurance coverage variability
- Veterans Administration programs
- Manufacturer assistance programs
- Research study participation
- International access disparities
