# Stimulation Devices

Stimulation devices deliver electrical, magnetic, or other forms of energy to specific body tissues to treat various medical conditions. These devices modulate neural activity, restore physiological functions, or provide therapeutic benefits through targeted stimulation.

## TENS Units for Pain Management

### Overview
Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulation (TENS) delivers low-voltage electrical current through electrodes placed on the skin to relieve pain.

### Mechanism of Action
- **Gate Control Theory**: Stimulation of large-diameter nerve fibers inhibits pain signal transmission
- **Endorphin Release**: May stimulate endogenous opioid production
- **Peripheral Nerve Modulation**: Alters nerve excitability and conduction

### Device Components
- Pulse generator unit
- Lead wires
- Surface electrodes (reusable or disposable)
- Battery power source

### Stimulation Parameters
| Parameter | Typical Range | Application |
|-----------|---------------|-------------|
| Frequency | 1-150 Hz | Low for chronic, high for acute pain |
| Pulse Width | 50-400 μs | Wider for deeper penetration |
| Intensity | 0-80 mA | Patient tolerance dependent |
| Waveform | Biphasic, monophasic | Biphasic preferred for comfort |

### Clinical Applications
- Chronic low back pain
- Osteoarthritis
- Fibromyalgia
- Neuropathic pain
- Post-operative pain management
- Labor pain

### Contraindications
- Cardiac pacemakers (relative)
- Pregnancy (abdominal/pelvic placement)
- Over carotid sinus
- Active malignancy (treatment area)
- Epilepsy (certain placements)

## Neurostimulators

### Spinal Cord Stimulation (SCS)

#### Overview
Implanted devices deliver electrical pulses to the spinal cord to interrupt pain signals.

#### Components
- Implantable pulse generator (IPG)
- Electrode leads (percutaneous or paddle)
- Patient programmer/remote
- Charging system (rechargeable units)

#### Indications
- Failed back surgery syndrome
- Complex regional pain syndrome (CRPS)
- Peripheral neuropathy
- Angina pectoris (refractory)
- Peripheral vascular disease

#### Stimulation Paradigms
- **Traditional SCS**: Paresthesia-based therapy
- **High-Frequency (HF10)**: 10 kHz, paresthesia-free
- **Burst Stimulation**: Packets of high-frequency pulses
- **Closed-Loop Systems**: Evoked compound action potential (ECAP) feedback

### Deep Brain Stimulation (DBS)

#### Overview
Electrodes implanted in specific brain nuclei deliver continuous electrical stimulation.

#### Target Structures
| Condition | Primary Target |
|-----------|----------------|
| Parkinson's Disease | Subthalamic nucleus (STN), GPi |
| Essential Tremor | Ventral intermediate nucleus (VIM) |
| Dystonia | Globus pallidus internus (GPi) |
| OCD | Ventral capsule/ventral striatum |
| Epilepsy | Anterior nucleus of thalamus |

#### System Components
- DBS leads with multiple contacts
- Extension cables
- Implantable pulse generator (chest/abdomen)
- Programming system

#### Surgical Considerations
- Stereotactic frame or frameless navigation
- Microelectrode recording for target localization
- Awake surgery vs. general anesthesia protocols

### Peripheral Nerve Stimulation (PNS)
- Occipital nerve (migraine, occipital neuralgia)
- Sacral nerve (bladder dysfunction, fecal incontinence)
- Vagus nerve (epilepsy, depression)
- Tibial nerve (overactive bladder)

## Pacemakers and Defibrillators

### Cardiac Pacemakers

#### Overview
Electronic devices that deliver electrical impulses to regulate heart rhythm in patients with bradyarrhythmias or conduction disorders.

#### Types by Chamber
- **Single-chamber**: Right atrium (AAI) or right ventricle (VVI)
- **Dual-chamber**: Atrium and ventricle (DDD)
- **Biventricular**: Cardiac resynchronization therapy (CRT-P)

#### Pacing Modes (NBG Code)
| Position | Category | Options |
|----------|----------|---------|
| I | Chamber paced | A, V, D, O |
| II | Chamber sensed | A, V, D, O |
| III | Response | T, I, D, O |
| IV | Rate modulation | R, O |
| V | Multisite pacing | A, V, D, O |

#### Key Features
- Rate-responsive pacing (activity sensors)
- Automatic mode switching
- Lead impedance monitoring
- Battery longevity indicators
- MRI-conditional designs

### Implantable Cardioverter-Defibrillators (ICD)

#### Overview
Devices that monitor heart rhythm and deliver therapy for life-threatening ventricular arrhythmias.

#### Therapy Tiers
1. **Antitachycardia Pacing (ATP)**: Overdrive pacing to terminate VT
2. **Cardioversion**: Low-energy synchronized shock
3. **Defibrillation**: High-energy shock for VF

#### Types
- **Transvenous ICD**: Traditional endocardial leads
- **Subcutaneous ICD (S-ICD)**: Entirely extravascular
- **CRT-D**: Defibrillator with biventricular pacing

#### Indications
- Secondary prevention (survived sudden cardiac arrest)
- Primary prevention (high-risk cardiomyopathy)
- Channelopathies (long QT, Brugada syndrome)

### Leadless Pacemakers
- Self-contained capsule devices
- Transcatheter implantation
- Right ventricular placement
- Examples: Medtronic Micra, Abbott Aveir

## Cochlear Implants

### Overview
Electronic devices that bypass damaged hair cells to directly stimulate the auditory nerve, providing sound perception for individuals with severe to profound sensorineural hearing loss.

### System Components

#### External Components
- Microphone (captures sound)
- Speech processor (analyzes and encodes)
- Transmitter coil (sends signal transcutaneously)

#### Internal Components
- Receiver-stimulator (implanted behind ear)
- Electrode array (inserted into cochlea)
- Ground electrode

### Signal Processing Strategies
- **CIS (Continuous Interleaved Sampling)**: Sequential electrode activation
- **SPEAK (Spectral Peak)**: Frequency-based electrode selection
- **ACE (Advanced Combination Encoder)**: Hybrid approach
- **FSP (Fine Structure Processing)**: Enhanced pitch perception

### Surgical Procedure
1. Mastoidectomy approach
2. Facial recess (posterior tympanotomy)
3. Cochleostomy or round window insertion
4. Electrode array advancement
5. Receiver-stimulator fixation

### Candidacy Criteria
- Severe to profound bilateral sensorineural hearing loss
- Limited benefit from hearing aids
- No medical contraindications
- Appropriate expectations and support system
- Children: typically 9-12 months minimum age

### Outcomes and Considerations
- Speech perception improvement (variable)
- Music appreciation limitations
- Ongoing programming/mapping sessions
- MRI compatibility considerations
- Bilateral implantation benefits

### Manufacturers
- Cochlear Limited (Nucleus)
- Advanced Bionics (HiRes)
- MED-EL (Synchrony)
- Oticon Medical (Neuro)

## Other Stimulation Technologies

### Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation (TMS)
- Non-invasive brain stimulation
- FDA-cleared for depression, OCD, migraine
- Repetitive TMS (rTMS) protocols

### Transcranial Direct Current Stimulation (tDCS)
- Low-intensity constant current
- Research and emerging clinical applications
- Neuromodulation without implantation

### Electroconvulsive Therapy (ECT) Devices
- Controlled seizure induction
- Treatment-resistant depression
- Modern brief-pulse and ultra-brief pulse techniques

### Bone Growth Stimulators
- Electromagnetic field stimulation
- Fracture healing enhancement
- Spinal fusion adjunct therapy

## Safety and Electromagnetic Interference

### Common Concerns
- MRI compatibility (conditional vs. non-conditional)
- Security systems and metal detectors
- Electrocautery during surgery
- Therapeutic radiation
- Cellular phones and electronic devices

### Patient Education
- Device identification cards
- Activity restrictions
- Emergency procedures
- Follow-up schedule compliance
- Battery/generator replacement timeline
