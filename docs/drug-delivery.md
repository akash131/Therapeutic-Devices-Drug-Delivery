# Drug Delivery Systems

Drug delivery devices are therapeutic instruments designed to administer medications to patients in controlled, precise doses. These systems optimize drug efficacy while minimizing side effects through targeted delivery mechanisms.

## Insulin Pumps

### Overview
Insulin pumps are wearable devices that deliver continuous subcutaneous insulin infusion (CSII) to patients with diabetes mellitus.

### Components
- **Reservoir**: Holds insulin supply (typically 2-3 day capacity)
- **Pump mechanism**: Precision motor-driven delivery system
- **Infusion set**: Tubing and cannula for subcutaneous delivery
- **Control unit**: Microprocessor with user interface

### Key Features
- Basal rate programming (continuous background insulin)
- Bolus delivery (mealtime or correction doses)
- Integration with continuous glucose monitors (CGM)
- Closed-loop systems (artificial pancreas)

### Clinical Considerations
- Improved glycemic control vs. multiple daily injections
- Reduced hypoglycemia risk with sensor-augmented systems
- Patient education and training requirements
- Site rotation to prevent lipohypertrophy

### Notable Systems
- Medtronic MiniMed series
- Tandem t:slim X2
- Omnipod (tubeless patch pump)
- Insulet systems

## Infusion Pumps

### Overview
Infusion pumps deliver fluids, medications, or nutrients into a patient's circulatory system in controlled amounts.

### Types

#### Large Volume Pumps (LVP)
- Deliver fluids at rates >100 mL/hr
- Used for IV fluids, parenteral nutrition
- Typical hospital bedside devices

#### Syringe Pumps
- High-precision, low-volume delivery
- Critical care and anesthesia applications
- Neonatal and pediatric use

#### Ambulatory Pumps
- Portable devices for outpatient use
- Chemotherapy, pain management, antibiotic therapy
- Patient-controlled analgesia (PCA)

#### Elastomeric Pumps
- Disposable, non-electronic
- Pressure-driven delivery
- Home infusion applications

### Safety Features
- Air-in-line detection
- Occlusion alarms
- Free-flow prevention
- Dose error reduction systems (DERS)
- Drug library integration

### Regulatory Standards
- IEC 60601-2-24 (Infusion pumps)
- FDA guidance on infusion pump safety

## Nebulizers and Inhalers

### Nebulizers

#### Jet Nebulizers
- Compressed gas drives medication aerosolization
- Affordable, widely available
- Lower efficiency, longer treatment times

#### Ultrasonic Nebulizers
- Piezoelectric crystal generates aerosol
- Quieter operation
- May heat medications (not suitable for all drugs)

#### Mesh Nebulizers
- Vibrating mesh technology
- Highly efficient delivery
- Portable, battery-powered options
- Examples: Philips InnoSpire, PARI eFlow

### Inhalers

#### Metered-Dose Inhalers (MDI)
- Pressurized canister with propellant
- Requires coordination for proper use
- Spacer devices improve delivery

#### Dry Powder Inhalers (DPI)
- Breath-actuated, no propellant
- Requires adequate inspiratory flow
- Various designs (single-dose, multi-dose)

#### Soft Mist Inhalers
- Slow-moving aerosol cloud
- Improved lung deposition
- Example: Boehringer Ingelheim Respimat

### Clinical Applications
- Asthma and COPD management
- Cystic fibrosis therapy
- Pulmonary infections
- Pulmonary arterial hypertension

## Transdermal Patch Systems

### Overview
Transdermal drug delivery systems (TDDS) administer medications through the skin for systemic absorption.

### Mechanisms

#### Passive Diffusion Patches
- Drug reservoir or matrix design
- Rate-controlling membrane
- Adhesive layer for skin contact

#### Active Enhancement Systems
- Iontophoresis (electrical current)
- Sonophoresis (ultrasound)
- Microneedle arrays
- Thermal ablation

### Advantages
- Avoids first-pass metabolism
- Steady-state plasma levels
- Improved patient compliance
- Non-invasive administration

### Limitations
- Limited to lipophilic, low-molecular-weight drugs
- Skin irritation potential
- Variable absorption rates
- Slower onset of action

| Rotigotine | Parkinson's disease | Neupro |

## Implantable Drug Reservoirs

### Overview
Implantable drug delivery systems provide long-term, controlled release of medications from surgically placed devices.

### Types

#### Passive Implants
- Biodegradable polymers (PLGA, PLA)
- Silicone reservoir systems
- Osmotic pumps

#### Active Implants
- Programmable pumps
- Electronically controlled release
- Refillable reservoirs

### Intrathecal Drug Delivery Systems
- Direct spinal cord medication delivery
- Chronic pain management
- Spasticity treatment (baclofen)
- Example: Medtronic SynchroMed II

### Ocular Implants
- Sustained release for eye diseases
- Reduces injection frequency
- Examples: Ozurdex (dexamethasone), Iluvien (fluocinolone)

### Contraceptive Implants
- Long-acting reversible contraception
- Subdermal rod placement
- Examples: Nexplanon, Implanon

### Oncology Applications
- Chemotherapy wafers (Gliadel for brain tumors)
- Brachytherapy seed implants
- Targeted local delivery

### Considerations
- Surgical placement and removal
- Infection risk management
- Device longevity and refill requirements
- MRI compatibility
